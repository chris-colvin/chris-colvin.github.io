# Dublin local taxation: HC 1822 (394)


## Figures

- Printed p.14, Thomas Ellis evidence, 29 March 1822: fixed taxes for year ending 31 December 1820, assessed £120,046 14s 3d; actually collected £94,837 1s 9d; uncollected £25,209 12s 6d. Visually verified. Collection about 79% of assessment.
- Printed p.13: city grand-jury cess in 1820 £24,533 11s 1d. This is presented in a series of assessments, not separately verified cash receipts. Do not add to cash fixed-tax collections and call the result actual revenue.
- Printed pp.13-14: criminal-jurisdiction expenses in 1820 £21,508 2s 7d. Part of grand-jury expenditure, not an additional levy.
- Currency of aggregate tax amounts is not explicitly identified in the relevant evidence. Retain as reported; do not label sterling or consolidate with central sterling accounts pending verification.

## Coverage and interpretation

Fixed taxes and grand-jury levies are distinguished (p.3; pp.11-14). Fixed services include paving/lighting, pipe water, metal mains, watch, wide streets, Foundling Hospital, cards/clubs. Quay-wall tax discussed separately on pp.17-18; its inclusion in the fixed-tax aggregate needs checking.

Some fixed taxes extended into county Dublin where services extended; city grand-jury jurisdiction was narrower (p.13). Do not assume identical geographic coverage.

Minister's money was the valuation basis for local taxes (pp.4,11-12), but this does not make local collections church revenue. No standalone church-revenue total established by this source.

Appendix reproduces earlier water accounts and 1819 audit material (pp.33 onward); sinking-fund figures extending through 1822 and later are calculations/projections, not observed annual 1822 tax receipts (pp.64,71 onward).

## Adopted estimate, 14 November 2026

Use 1820 receipts as a proxy for 1822, as requested. £94,837 1s 9d minus wide-street receipts of £4,944 7s 6d (city) and £856 2s 6d (county), and a precautionary deduction of £1,812 18s 11d for Anna Liffey collections, gives £87,223.63 in reported pounds. The quay deduction is conservative: inclusion in the fixed-tax aggregate is uncertain. Assuming Irish currency and multiplying by 12/13 gives £80,514.12 sterling. Adopt £80,000, rounded down. Ordinary Dublin grand-jury cess is separate and is not added again. This is an estimate, not a fully reconciled net receipt or a national municipal total.
