# Currency audit: grand-jury return, 1822


Tyrone is excluded throughout, as instructed. No imputation.

## Findings

- Antrim, p.4: 1810–1825 explicitly late Irish currency; later figures British. Convert 1822 by 12/13.
- Drogheda, p.10: 1810–1825 subtotal explicitly Irish, followed by its conversion into British currency. Annual 1822 entry is £1,067 2s 3½d. Convert by 12/13; the abstract repeats its unconverted whole pounds.
- Limerick county, p.20: bracket explicitly marks 1810–1825 as late Irish currency. 1822 £31,763 2s 11d. Convert by 12/13.
- Limerick city, p.21: bracket explicitly marks pre-1826 entries Irish currency. 1822 £5,130 13s 11½d. Convert by 12/13.
- Cork city, p.8: British designation begins at 1826. This strongly suggests earlier entries are Irish but does not explicitly label them so. Treat 1822 as probably Irish, not confirmed.
- Sligo, p.27: total explicitly labelled present currency. Re-adding all twenty annual entries in pounds, shillings and pence gives exactly the printed £326,480 19s 2d. No conversion is made between the annual entries and that total. Strong evidence that annual entries, including 1822 £15,713 15s 4d, are already sterling. Retain unchanged.

Remaining returns do not supply an explicit currency designation in the extracted text. Visually checked image-only tables for King's County p.18, Louth p.23 and Wicklow p.29: no currency designation. Donegal p.9 likewise unlabelled. Unlabelled figures cannot safely be assigned sterling merely because the return was compiled in 1830, nor Irish solely because the year is 1822.

## Currency sensitivity calculation

Use previously transcribed abstract whole-pound entries, sum 706344. Confirmed Irish entries sum 72467; Sligo 15713; Cork city 18925. Whole-pound abstraction omits shillings/pence; round outputs rather than imply precision.

- Convert confirmed Irish entries, retain Sligo, assume every unresolved entry sterling: 706344 - 72467/13 = £700,769.62.
- Retain Sligo, assume every other entry Irish: (706344 - 15713)*12/13 + 15713 = £653,218.54.
- If probable Irish Cork city is also converted, the upper scenario becomes £699,313.85.

Thus £0.65–0.70 million sterling is a useful currency-only working range for reported grand-jury amounts excluding Tyrone. It is not a statistical confidence interval, a verified point estimate, or a bound on all local revenue/spending. Do not describe the original £706,344 as sterling, and do not convert the entire total uniformly.

## Other limitations retained

Mayo p.24 includes spring 1822 assessments substantially collected in later years: these are not uniform cash receipts. Municipal and other local levies require separate reconciliation to avoid overlap. Grand-jury totals alone do not measure total local spending. Do not combine with central accounts without matching currency and accounting coverage.

Next evidence needed for a point estimate: currency-labelled contemporary accounts or reproductions for the unresolved jurisdictions, especially high-value Cork county, Tipperary, Galway county and Down. The current return itself supports a range more securely than a single exact sterling total.
