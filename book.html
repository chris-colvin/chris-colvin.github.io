After the Union: Ireland Inside the World’s First Industrial Economy, 1801–1901

Christopher L. Colvin and Eoin McLaughlin

This book examines the economic consequences of Ireland’s political union with Great Britain in the nineteenth century. We argue that the timing of the Union mattered as much as its terms. Joining an already industrialising economy reinforced patterns of specialisation that proved difficult to change. Earlier economic structures and institutions shaped how common policies worked in different local settings.

The Great Famine, mass emigration and institutional reform reshaped Irish society. Living standards converged towards British levels, but structural transformation remained limited. Written for readers interested in Irish history and economic change, the book combines economic history scholarship with cliometric analysis to explain Ireland’s first century within the United Kingdom.

Publication is scheduled for 2027 with Cambridge University Press. Currently on seminar tour.
