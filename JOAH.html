Journal of Applied History economic history audit

Volumes 1–8, labelled 2019–2026. Screening date: 27 October 2026. Prepared for reviewing the scope of applied economic history.

6 of 55 articles and essays (10.9%) have a clear economic-history focus. A further 9 are adjacent or borderline. Including all nine gives 15 of 55 (27.3%), but that broader figure includes work whose main purpose is public health, environmental policy or political cohesion.

The six clear contributions concern applied economics and economic history; veterans’ pensions and racial exclusion; globalisation and US–China rivalry; historical reasoning during the financial crisis; US–China resource dependence; and commercial reform in seventeenth-century Catalonia. The catalogue below gives titles, authors, sources and an individual classification for every record.

Interpretation: This is an explicit title-and-abstract screening, with selected full-text checks, not a full-text content analysis of every article. The counts are reproducible classification judgements. They support “a minority of contributions have an explicitly economic-history focus”; they should not be presented as an official journal statistic or a definitive disciplinary boundary.

Scope and counting rules

The inventory contains 63 authored contributions from the publisher’s Crossref journal feed for ISSN 2589-5893. Front and back matter have been removed from the inventory and all counts. Each DOI is counted once, including items first published online before their assigned volume year. Volume years are used for the annual table; the recorded publication date is retained separately. The first volume is labelled 2019. All retrieved publication dates precede the screening date. The publisher’s tables of contents for volumes 3–6 were also consulted; this does not independently rule out unregistered items in other volumes.

Main denominator: 55 articles and essays, including methodological and review essays. Excluded from this denominator: 3 editorials, 3 issue introductions, and 2 response articles in the adoption debate. These eight authored contributions remain visible below. They are analytic counting rules, not a claim that every included piece has the same publisher article type.

Clear economic history: the principal question or a substantial application concerns historical economic processes, economic policymaking, commercial projects, resources and dependence, or welfare distribution. An economic historian as author, a quantitative method, or incidental economic context is insufficient.

Adjacent or borderline: historical education, health regulation, energy policy, infrastructure, land use, resource governance, systemic collapse or economic reform overlaps with economic history, but the available evidence does not establish it as the central inquiry. This is a deliberately inclusive sensitivity category. General pandemic history, colonial history and political history are not automatically included.

Denominator sensitivity: including the two adoption responses gives 6/57 (10.5%) clear and 15/57 (26.3%) clear plus adjacent. Including the substantial strategic-communication introduction as well gives 6/58 (10.3%) and 15/58 (25.9%). Including all authored contributions, including editorials and introductions, gives 6/63 (9.5%) and 15/63 (23.8%).

Classification sensitivity: the Roman-collapse and land-use articles are particularly plausible candidates for promotion from adjacent after a full-text assessment. Promoting both would give 8/55 (14.5%) clear. Treating the pension article as medical history only would instead reduce the clear count to 5/55 (9.1%); its substantive treatment of pension provision is why it is included here.
